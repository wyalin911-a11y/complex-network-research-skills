# Research Patterns for Upgrading Complex-Network Ideas

Use as search lenses, not recipes.

## 1. Competing mechanisms
Opposing mechanisms may create reentrance, non-monotonicity, coexistence, hysteresis,
or multiple transitions. Examples: failure vs recovery, redundancy vs dependency,
local support vs global connectivity.

## 2. Correlated heterogeneity
Study correlations standard independent assignments erase:
degree↔threshold, hyperedge-size↔vulnerability, centrality↔recovery,
dependency-strength↔repair priority. Ask whether correlation changes phase structure.

## 3. Local–global mismatch
Separate local stability, giant-component membership, accessibility, and system-level function.
Look for hidden local robustness with global failure or intervention tradeoffs.

## 4. Averaging failure
Test whether replacing heterogeneity by its mean shifts a boundary, changes transition order,
reverses a policy conclusion, or erases a phase.

## 5. Endogenous adaptation
Let state affect structure: failure → rewiring/resource redistribution → new vulnerability.
Look for feedback-induced phase changes.

## 6. Time-scale competition
Compare failure, repair, adaptation, and information time scales.
Look for delayed collapse, recovery-induced instability, or non-monotonic resilience.

## 7. Higher-order dependency semantics
Use hypergraphs only when group-level dependence loses essential information under
pairwise projection.

## 8. Control paradox
Interventions may backfire: extra redundancy concentrates load, faster recovery synchronizes
failure, targeted protection shifts risk elsewhere. Require mechanism + falsifiable boundary.

## 9. Multi-branch fixed-point structure
When self-consistency equations have multiple extrema/branches, search for saddle nodes,
tricritical points, branch recruitment, hysteresis, and continuous/discontinuous changes.

## 10. Theory–decision bridge
After a mechanism is established, ask whether it induces a real decision problem:
reinforcement, repair allocation, dependency redesign, or resilience-efficiency tradeoff.
