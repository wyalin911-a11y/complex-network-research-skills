# Novelty Rubric for Complex-Network Research

## Four levels of novelty
1. Surface novelty: different topology, dataset, parameter, or application.
2. Model novelty: new combination or rule.
3. Mechanism novelty: scientifically motivated process changes collective behavior.
4. Discovery novelty: previously unknown, explainable emergent phenomenon.

Strong projects should preferably reach levels 3–4.

## Score guide

### 0–2: Repackaging
- same method on a new dataset
- parameter tuning
- cosmetic network substitution

### 3–4: Incremental extension
- known model with one added feature
- heterogeneous version with only quantitative shifts
- another attack/recovery strategy with expected results

### 5–6: Meaningful model contribution
- justified mechanism
- nontrivial quantitative consequences
- improved explanation/prediction
- limited conceptual discovery

### 7–8: Strong mechanism/discovery
- qualitative behavior changes
- new transition structure
- paradox or non-monotonicity
- analytical explanation plausible
- clear difference from closest prior art

### 9–10: Exceptional conceptual novelty
Reserve for verified ideas that could:
- reveal a genuinely new general mechanism
- overturn a common assumption
- establish a new critical phenomenon
- provide a clean theoretical principle
- survive serious prior-art search

Do not assign 9+ before literature verification.

## Required novelty questions
1. What is the closest prior model?
2. What is the closest prior phenomenon?
3. What exact assumption changes?
4. Why is that assumption scientifically justified?
5. Does it create qualitatively new behavior?
6. Can the behavior be isolated by ablation?
7. Can theory explain it?
8. Is the contribution still interesting if the headline phenomenon is absent?
9. Is the result robust across a region rather than a single point?
10. Is the novelty a new mechanism or merely a new combination?

## Red flags
Downgrade when the novelty claim depends mainly on:
- "nobody combined X and Y"
- "first application of X to Y"
- "we introduce parameter alpha"
- "we compare more network models"
- "robustness changes with parameters"

These may be paper components, but are not sufficient top-level novelty claims.
